ACRP vs SOCRA Certification: Which Clinical Research Exam Should You Take?

By Ish, ACRP-CP • March 2026 • 8 min read

If you're a clinical research professional weighing certification options, you've probably landed on two names: ACRP (Association of Clinical Research Professionals) and SOCRA (Society of Clinical Research Associates). Both are widely recognized, but they differ in meaningful ways that can affect your study approach, your wallet, and your career trajectory.

Here's a clear, honest comparison to help you decide — and prepare — for the right exam.

Exam Content: The Biggest Difference

This is where your study strategy will diverge significantly. The ACRP-CP exam is built entirely around the ICH-GCP guidelines — the international framework. You won't be asked about specific U.S. federal regulations or FDA forms. The exam assumes you can map ICH principles to any country's regulatory system.

The SOCRA CCRP exam casts a wider net. In addition to ICH-GCP, it tests your knowledge of U.S. Code of Federal Regulations (21 CFR), FDA-specific requirements, and the Declaration of Helsinki. This makes the SOCRA exam broader in scope, which some candidates find more challenging — there's simply more material to cover.

What Does This Mean for Your Study Plan?

If you're taking the ACRP exam, your study time should be laser-focused on ICH E6 principles, investigator and sponsor responsibilities, and how to apply those concepts in scenario-based questions. If you're taking SOCRA, you'll also need dedicated time for FDA reporting timelines, CFR-specific requirements, and the ethical frameworks that underpin U.S. clinical research.

Difficulty: What Candidates Say

Neither organization publishes official pass rates. Anecdotally, both exams are considered challenging, and most successful candidates report studying for 2–3 months. The consensus among CRCs who've taken both is that the SOCRA exam feels broader while the ACRP exam goes deeper on ICH application. Some prefer one format over the other based on their work experience.

The SOCRA exam's use of case-based questions can be demanding — you're tested on your ability to apply regulations to realistic scenarios, not just recall facts. The ACRP exam is similarly scenario-heavy, which is why practice questions that mirror this format are essential for either exam.

Cost Comparison

SOCRA certification tends to be less expensive overall. ACRP exam fees are generally higher, especially for non-members. Both organizations offer member discounts, and both require continuing education investments for recertification. Factor in the cost of study materials as well — bootcamps can run $500–$700+, while self-study options with question banks are significantly more affordable.

Which Should You Choose?

Choose ACRP if: you want a role-specific certification (especially CCRC or CCRA), you work in an international setting or with global sponsors, or your organization specifically values ACRP credentials.

Choose SOCRA if: you want a single, unified credential that covers all clinical research roles, you primarily work in U.S.-based research, or you prefer flexible testing windows.

The good news: the core knowledge — ICH-GCP, ethics, clinical operations, safety reporting — overlaps heavily between both exams. If you build a strong foundation in these areas, you're preparing for either certification simultaneously.

How to Prepare for Either Exam

Regardless of which certification you choose, the most effective preparation follows the same formula: master the foundational guidelines, then test yourself relentlessly with scenario-based practice questions. Reading alone isn't enough — you need to practice applying knowledge under timed conditions.
